# menzil threat model (spec v0.4, 2026-11-07)

Status: v0.4 adds public shares as built (the relay principal in 2.3, the front door and the HTTP terminator in 2.7, name to network attribution in 2.10, tests 22 to 29 in 5, rewritten tests 6, 11 and 13) and states what is not built. v0.3 added the hostile tenant of a shared relay (2.10, with a non goal in 4 and tests 15 to 19 in 5) and NodeCert renewal (2.5, 2.6, test 5 reworded, tests 20 and 21) to v0.2, which followed the red team review of v0.1. Companion to `protocol.md` v0.4; section references point there.

## Changes in v0.4 (expose)

- 2.3: the relay principal is an L4 initiator with its own session; what it may open and what it may forge (`meta`) are stated, and that a blind share's content stays unreadable while the relay is passive.
- 2.7: the unknown name no longer gets a static site but one fixed seven byte alert; the peek flood, refusal indistinguishability (and its timing limit), HTTP request smuggling against terminated shares and ACME abuse are covered with their controls and residual risks.
- 2.10: a share name resolves to a network only inside its tenant's zone, share traffic is counted per tenant, and a terminated share cannot set a cookie for a parent domain.
- 5: tests 6, 11 and 13 describe what the code does; tests 22 to 29 are new.
- 6: open items.

## 1. What we protect

| Asset | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Contents of every private stream and datagram (S2 reach, S3 egress) | The core promise: only the two endpoints read them |
| Contents of blind public shares (S1, default once shipped) | The relay operator must not be able to read a share it hosts by observing it |
| Node identities and the ability to act as one | A stolen or forged identity opens every service granted to it |
| Membership and policy: who may reach what | Wrong grants are the most likely real world failure |
| Grants, device names and egress rules (the Policy) | Hidden from relays; the Roster the relay sees holds ids only |
| Service existence and topology (which nodes exist, which shares exist) | Node ids and share names are visible to the relay and, for shares, to the public (certificate transparency) |
| Relay availability | A personal relay is a single point of failure; abuse must not take it down |

## 2. Adversaries and what each can do

### 2.1 Passive network observer (ISP, campus, wifi)

Sees: TLS connections from the node to the relay's domain on 443, their timing and sizes, DNS lookups of the relay name unless DoH is used. Cannot see anything inside. Not a goal: hiding that the relay domain is contacted or that a long lived WebSocket exists; traffic analysis resistance is out of scope. Version 1 key agreement is X25519 without a post quantum component, so an observer who records traffic today could decrypt it with a future quantum computer; a hybrid is a version 2 change.

### 2.2 Inspecting corporate proxy (TLS interception with a trusted enterprise CA)

Sees: the HTTP upgrade request, the WebSocket framing, the L3 records as ciphertext, record sizes and timing, the relay host name and the CONNECT target. Because L3 is a Noise session keyed to the relay's identity, the proxy cannot read control records or forwarded payloads, impersonate the relay, or inject records. It can block, delay, throttle or drop. It can record a HELLO and replay it; the ATTACH rule and the monotonic timestamp make a replayed HELLO harmless (section 4.1). Requirement met by design: the outer TLS is verified against the OS trust store and never pinned, so interception does not hard fail. Not a goal: passing as browser traffic under fingerprinting (review section 4.5).

### 2.3 Malicious or compromised relay

Sees: node identities and NodeCerts, the Rosters of the networks it serves (ids, revocations, minimum serials, label claims, stewards), which nodes are online, who sends to whom and when, record sizes, advertised names, and for terminated mode shares the full HTTP plaintext, by design. Does not see: Policies (grants, device names, egress rules travel end to end only), or any L4 payload.

Cannot: read or forge L4 records (authenticated encryption with contiguous counters, so a dropped, duplicated or reordered reliable record ends the session instead of corrupting a stream); impersonate a member to another member (the L4 handshake binds dialed NodeId, RECV source and prologue to the NodeCert); mint members or alter documents (owner and steward signatures; the admission tag is keyed with a secret the relay never sees); freeze revocation indefinitely (documents expire within 14 days and peers exchange sequence numbers end to end); open arbitrary streams to a node (as the relay principal it opens streams only to services of shares that the node advertised, the relay accepted and the network's Roster grants the node, over an L4 session whose initiator key must equal the static key of the L3 session of that attachment, so a member cannot pose as the relay, and everything else, `menzil:docs` and the services the node serves to members included, is refused `no_grant`; the node judges its own standing and its share set at every OPEN); take documents from the node or push any to it on that session (none is served there); disconnect a node by replaying its HELLO.

Can: drop or delay traffic, refuse service, lie about peer presence, and observe the metadata above. As the relay principal it can also open streams to the shared services of any node that advertised a share it accepted, up to 64 per share and 256 per node (protocol.md 6.1), which is the bound of what it can make a node's local targets do; and it can forge `meta`, the client address and SNI it passes with an OPEN. The node validates the form of `meta` and the SNI against its share names, and `meta` is information for logs and for the service, never authorization; a service behind a share that trusts the client address is trusting the relay operator. For a terminated share the relay also reads and rewrites the HTTP and chooses the `X-Forwarded-For` the service sees. An operator who also controls the DNS of the share domain can obtain certificates for blind names and terminate them; this is detectable through certificate transparency and preventable for custom domains with CAA `accounturi`. Blind therefore means blind against a passive relay. Mitigation available to users: run your own relay, attach to several, use a custom domain with CAA.

### 2.4 Malicious member

Can: open L4 sessions to members of the same network and attempt any stream; send datagrams up to its token bucket; initiate handshakes up to the per peer limit. Stopped by: default deny grants scoped to that network and enforced at the target node, grant re evaluation on policy change, egress deny lists at the exit, per channel token buckets, per peer handshake limits, L3 credit so that one slow or hostile peer cannot stall others, and revocation. Cannot: read traffic between other members (pairwise keys), impersonate the owner or steward, escalate through roles of another network (principals are scoped), or reach services not granted. Residual risk: a member with an egress grant uses the exit's address for its traffic, bounded by the exit's rules and by revocation.

### 2.5 Stolen node key

The holder is that node until revoked. Both secret keys live in one file, and a holder of the Ed25519 key can sign a NodeCert with any serial, which the relay judges as presented (protocol.md 4.1); so a higher `min_serial` keeps no thief out, and the response to a key that may be compromised is revocation, with the device joining again under a new identity (`menzil init --new-identity`, `admit --replace`, protocol.md 7.3). A renewal (same keys, higher serial) answers expiry only. The relay judges a NodeCert's window at HELLO only, so a stolen key with an expired certificate keeps a relay session it already has; that is accepted, because the holder can sign a fresh certificate anyway, revocation ends the relay session, and peers end L4 sessions at the listed certificate's `not_after`. A holder of the Ed25519 key without the X25519 half can sign renewals for a key it cannot use, and cannot get another key listed: a steward lists only the key the Policy already lists (protocol.md 2.3), and the owner's `cert accept` refuses a new key. Mitigations: key files restricted to the user (mode 0600, Windows ACL), revocation through Roster and Policy applied by relays and peers on receipt, document expiry of 14 days as the upper bound for a relay that withholds updates from a verifier that holds no steward signed renewal (a steward renews from the base it holds, so a revocation that never reaches the steward lasts until the steward's `not_after`, at most 90 days; this is accepted: a steward key is trusted for its whole `not_after`, and a stolen steward key is a risk of up to 90 days, so keep stewards short lived and on devices that are online), one attached session per NodeId so the legitimate node notices displacement. Not in phase 1: hardware backed keys, device attestation.

### 2.6 Stolen owner key

Unrecoverable for that network: the attacker can sign documents and admit members. Remedy is a new network and re admission of devices. Mitigations: the owner key stays offline; the always online admission device holds a steward key limited to admit and renew with a lifetime of at most 90 days; one writer issues sequence numbers. A steward signed document must travel with its owner signed base and may only renew, append members with no roles, a unique name and a `min_serial` no higher than their own certificate's serial, or list a member's renewed certificate for the key the Policy already lists (protocol.md 2.3), so a stolen steward key cannot unrevoke, grant, claim labels, change stewards, change a `min_serial` or move a member of the owner's base to another key (a member it appended itself only at verifiers that hold none of its earlier listing of that member, and such a member has no grants); it cannot take back a renewal a verifier holds; a verifier that holds the steward's earlier admissions refuses a document that drops them, and a steward that may only admit cannot keep renewing what it admitted. A stolen steward key can still take the network down at once: it can sign a pair at the owner base `seq` plus 2^20 with a short `expires`, which every verifier then holds, and only the owner recovers the network (by signing a higher `seq`, which takes the steward out); it cannot outrank the owner.

### 2.7 Internet attacker against the relay

Attempts: connection floods, HELLO floods (an office behind one proxy shares one address, so per address limits are generous; a NodeId is bounded once the handshake has proven it, by the cap on its sessions waiting to attach, since a per NodeId HELLO rate is not built), SNI probing and name enumeration, slow or oversized ClientHellos (the peek flood), exhaustion of share connections, abuse of terminated shares as an open proxy or by HTTP request smuggling, ACME abuse, admission request floods.

Controls, all connections: rate limits per address, one attached session per NodeId, 65,535 byte records, bounded queues, label claims signed by owners with quotas and quarantine, pending admissions capped. Controls at the front door (protocol.md 6.2): the ClientHello is read within 16 KiB, 32 records and 10 seconds, at most 1,024 at a time, and a connection over the cap, a stalled one or one that closes is dropped without a byte; the parser refuses anything two parsers could read differently (a second `server_name`, a repeated extension, trailing bytes, a non DNS name), so the name the relay routes on is the one the node's TLS stack sees; every other failure (unknown, unheld, quarantined, wrong zone, wrong share kind, an `acme-tls/1` hello for a share that did not list it, any figure reached, a node that refuses or is silent) is answered by the same seven bytes after at least 250 milliseconds, so the answer does not tell which condition applied and a lookup in memory does not stand out from one that took a round trip. The names the relay routes are judged at every connection (held, granted by the Roster now, holder attached, network not held back, tenant and zone agree), not when they were accepted. Share connections are counted per name, per tenant, per client and per IPv6 /48, and in all (protocol.md 10), and a piped connection that never hears from the node is ended after 30 seconds. ACME routing and answering exist only for orders that are pending now (exact name, ALPN exactly `acme-tls/1`); a relay orders certificates only for names an accepted terminated share of a flagged tenant makes wanted, never for a name a client sent, and the order limiter allows one order per name per minute, with backoff to an hour, and 16 in flight (protocol.md 6.3). Controls for terminated shares: Host must equal the SNI (421), the relay's own strict HTTP/1.1 parser refuses ambiguous framing, bare line ends, control bytes, folded lines, `Expect` and `Upgrade`, strips every header that names the client and sets its own `X-Forwarded-For`, re-emits the framing in canonical form, and holds the node's response to the same rules (protocol.md 6.3); the relay never forwards a request it is unsure of, and a response that would split framing is a 502. Public shares are the one surface accepting unauthenticated connections; everything else requires a Noise handshake with a listed identity.

Residual risks, stated plainly:

- The refusal is identical in bytes, not in time when the cause takes long: a held name whose node is attached but does not answer the relay principal's handshake or OPEN holds the connection up to 10 seconds before the alert, which tells a prober that the name is held and its node is attached and silent.
- A name that a node serves is not hidden: a client that guesses it completes a TLS handshake with the node's own certificate (blind) or the relay's (terminated), so names can be enumerated by guessing, as certificate transparency allows anyway. The identical refusal prevents telling the unserved causes apart, not finding the served names.
- Refusals hold a socket for their 250 millisecond delay and up to half a second of draining, at most 1,024 at a time (over that a refusal is closed at once). The front door needs up to 7,168 descriptors on top of the relay's sessions, and the relay warns at start if its limit is lower; when the limit is reached the relay stops accepting connections, its nodes' reconnects included.
- An attacker with many IPv4 addresses holds up to 32 share connections per address (IPv6 /64s of one /48 together at most 128); the per client figures bound one source, and the figures per name, per tenant and in all (4,096) bound the sum, so a determined attacker with enough addresses can fill them and starve the public clients of a name. Share connections take no session place, so members' sessions are not counted in them, but they use descriptors (above).
- The per address rate of 60 new connections a minute applies to public share clients before their ClientHello is read, so many visitors behind one address (a school, a company) share it.
- The node does not know the relay's share domain, so the domain part of the `sni` in `meta` is not checked (`app.attacker.example` passes); `meta` is information only (2.3, TBD 53).
- The relay reads and rewrites terminated shares. Its HTTP parser is new code; the smuggling tests in section 5 cover what was thought of, not what a framing difference with a particular node server could still allow. A request body is passed to the node completely before the response is read, and header names reach the node in the case the client sent them.
- Certificate material: the relay's ACME account key and certificates are files in its directory; whoever reads them can impersonate the relay's names, and the CA the operator names is trusted for those names. A blind share's name is not in the relay's certificates.
- Not built (protocol.md 6.3, 6.2): a static site (so there is no page for a probe to read), ECH, `Upgrade` and HTTP/2 for terminated shares, access control on the relay side.

### 2.8 Wrong policy

The owner is trusted; mistakes are the realistic risk (`to_node: "*"` with `services: ["*:*"]`). The CLI prints every grant in words, default templates are narrow, and the default egress deny list is written into the Policy so that what is enforced is what was signed.

### 2.9 Malicious peer over a direct path (phase 3)

The direct path will most likely be QUIC with its own reliability; L4 version 1 records are defined for the relay path, where the reliable class requires contiguity and only datagrams tolerate loss. Whatever the direct path is, it must add no trust: same identities, same network scoping, same grants.

### 2.10 Hostile tenant on a shared relay

A relay may serve several unrelated tenants, each a group of networks its operator assigned (protocol.md 10). Every tenant trusts the operator exactly as 2.3 says; tenancy protects tenants from each other, not from the operator. The attackers: the owner of a tenant's network, who can sign any Roster for it (any members, any labels, any `seq`, up to its size figure) and run any number of devices; a member device of some tenant; and a stranger with a key it just generated, listed in no Roster. Their goals against a victim tenant: learn its networks, members, labels or which of its nodes are online; reach its nodes; take its share names; starve it of relay memory, CPU, bandwidth, sessions, handshake slots or log lines.

Controls (protocol.md section in brackets):

- Nothing names another tenant's networks. WELCOME lists only the networks the HELLO claimed and passed, and nothing in XX [4.1]; a network the relay does not serve is refused `not_member` like one that does not list the node, at the same cost [4.1]; DOC for a network the session did not claim, or the relay does not serve, gets no answer [4.3]; a HELLO with more than 32 networks is refused before any lookup [4.1]; WELCOME `session` numbers do not count other connections [4.1].
- Nothing reaches another tenant's node. Forwarding needs a network claimed by both sessions, so an owner who lists a victim's NodeId in its own Roster can send nothing to it; every case after authorization answers the same `peer_offline`, so the answer is no presence oracle [4.2]. A Roster's `min_serial` and `revoked` count only for the networks a node claimed, so one owner cannot lock a node out of another's network.
- Names live in one zone per tenant. A tenant can produce no name in another's zone, so there is nothing to race for and `label_taken` exists only inside one tenant; flat names exclude every slug; a released flat name and a removed tenant's slug are quarantined for 180 days, so links, bookmarks and certificate history of the old holder never land on a new tenant [4.4, 11].
- Every resource a tenant can use is bounded per tenant or per session: sessions attached and waiting, pairs, destinations per sender, queued droppable bytes, bandwidth and volume, Roster size and adoptions, names [10]; and per source address and per NodeId before any tenant is known: connection rate, handshakes in flight, sessions waiting to attach, the ATTACH deadline, write timeouts, the replay history bound [10]. A tenant over its bandwidth delays only its own CREDIT [4.2].
- A share name leads to a network and a node only inside its tenant's zone. At the front door the tenant recorded with the held name, the tenant the relay holds for the name's network now and the zone label of the name must all agree, or the name is refused with the one alert; a network that changed tenant since a name was accepted therefore routes nothing. Share connections, principal sessions (256 per tenant) and principal streams (1,024 per tenant) are counted per tenant, and a principal session's frames are charged to the bandwidth and volume of the tenant of the name that opened it, not to a pair [6.1, 6.2, 10]. A terminated share is served only for a tenant the operator flagged `terminated`, under that tenant's own zone (`<label>.<slug>.t.H`), and the relay's certificates are for exact names, never a wildcard that could cover another tenant's [6.3].
- No lock that other tenants need is held across a signature check or an ADVERTISE decision; the log lines a peer can cause are throttled per peer, so that no peer floods the operator's log or hides another's first warning.

Residual risks, accepted:

- Until the share domain is on the Public Suffix List and browsers carry the listing, a blind share of one tenant can set cookies for the share domain that browsers send to other tenants' shares (cookie tossing); the relay cannot filter them because it cannot read a blind share. A terminated share cannot: the relay removes from a `Set-Cookie` every `Domain` attribute except the share's own exact name, so such a cookie stays host only (protocol.md 6.3). One tenant's abuse, phishing on a share for example, can get the share domain blocklisted for every tenant; the operator's remedy is to remove that tenant.
- A session counts against the tenant of every network it claims and is refused while any of them is at a session figure. A device that joined a hostile owner's network as well as the victim's can therefore be kept off the victim's network too, for as long as the hostile owner keeps its own tenant full. Listing the device in a Roster is not enough for this: a device claims only the networks it joined, and one that leaves the hostile network out of its HELLO is unaffected.
- The default tenant has no tenant figures and flat names. A friend's network belongs in a named tenant; a network left in the default tenant is limited only by the global limits.
- ERROR `over_quota` for a new pair names no destination, so a refused node cannot tell which peer it was; the figures are meant to be reached only by abuse. A relay restart begins a new volume period; only the operator can cause one.
- Inside one tenant there is no isolation beyond fair sharing of delayed CREDIT: its members share its figures, and one of them can use them up for the others. The tenant's owner admitted that member.

## 3. Guarantees by layer

| Layer | Confidentiality against | Authenticity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 outer TLS | passive observer | relay domain unless intercepted | OS trust store, never pinned, 1.2 accepted |
| L3 relay session | observer, inspecting proxy | node and relay, mutually (Noise IK or XX anchored in the relay NodeId) | Control plane; the relay sees Rosters and metadata by design |
| L4 end to end | observer, proxy, relay | peers, bound to dialed id, source and network | Reliable class contiguous; datagram class windowed |
| L5 streams | inherited from L4 | authorization at the target node, re evaluated on policy change; exit rules for egress | OPEN before any byte |
| Blind share | observer, proxy, passive relay | the certificate of the service behind the share, to the browser | Active operator with DNS control can misissue; CT and CAA |
| Terminated share | observer, proxy | relay certificate (exact name, ACME) to the browser; node to relay via L4 | The relay reads and rewrites HTTP by design; opt in per share and per tenant |

## 4. Explicit non goals

- Anonymity of nodes toward relays, or of relays toward the network.
- Resistance to traffic analysis and fingerprinting by the local network operator.
- Protection against a malicious owner, or recovery from a stolen owner key.
- Hiding share names; they are public through certificate transparency, and a served name answers a client that guesses it (2.7).
- Keeping a terminated share's content from the relay operator: the relay reads and rewrites it by design (2.3).
- Availability under a determined denial of service against a personal relay.
- Post quantum confidentiality in version 1.
- Hiding that menzil is in use: ordinary TLS carrying an ordinary WebSocket to a personal domain, nothing more.
- Isolation of tenants from the relay operator: on a shared relay the operator sees every tenant's Rosters, NodeIds, traffic metadata, label claims and terminated shares, as 2.3 says; tenancy isolates tenants from each other only (2.10).

## 5. Security requirements the implementation must test

1. A relay that drops, duplicates or reorders one reliable L4 record causes the receiver to end the session; no stream ever delivers a hole or reordered bytes. Datagram records inside the window are accepted, outside it dropped.
2. An admission request replayed by the relay with a different NodeCert is rejected by the steward (tag mismatch); a request with the correct tag succeeds exactly `uses` times across any number of relays.
3. A member of network N2 cannot use a role of N2 to reach a service granted to that role name in N1 on a node that belongs to both.
4. A revoked node is refused by relays and peers on receipt of the new Roster, and its existing relay and L4 sessions are ended then; a peer that cannot obtain documents newer than `expires` refuses new full L4 sessions and serves nothing but `menzil:docs` on the ones it has; it still completes docs only sessions, so that newer documents can reach it.
5. A responder NodeCert whose `node_id` differs from the dialed NodeId, or an initiator whose `node_id` differs from the RECV source, aborts the L4 handshake. A NodeCert with a serial below `min_serial` is rejected at L3 and L4, and one below the highest the relay has accepted for that NodeId since it started is rejected at L3. At L4 a lower serial is judged against the verifier's Policy (protocol.md 2.2): a listed certificate below a higher one of the same node that is valid now never backs a session, a session key that is not the key of that highest certificate is refused, and the serial of a certificate a peer presents is never used.
6. The relay principal cannot open `udp:`, `egress:*`, `menzil:docs` or a service the node serves only to members, nor the service of a share the node did not advertise, the relay did not accept or the network's Roster does not grant (each is `no_grant`, and nothing reaches the local target); it opens `tls:`, `http:` and `tcp:` services of accepted shares and `acme-http:` for a blind name only; `meta` from a member is ignored, and `meta` from the relay is validated and never authorizes anything.
7. A replayed HELLO does not supersede an attached session and is rejected by the timestamp rule; a new session becomes routable only after ATTACH.
8. With one slow destination, other destinations of the same source keep flowing; a reliable SEND beyond credit closes the sending session; droppable records are dropped first under pressure.
9. Two `menzil stdio` processes for one user share one agent and one relay session, and both SSH sessions stay up.
10. Egress: a name resolving to a denied address is refused before any dial; `0.0.0.0`, IPv4 mapped loopback, `100.64.0.0/10` and the exit's own addresses are denied by the default list; a UDP channel never sends to an address other than the OPEN target.
11. A request to a terminated share with a `Host` that differs from the SNI receives 421; client supplied `Forwarded`, `X-Forwarded-*` and `X-Real-IP` never reach the node, which sees one `X-Forwarded-For` with the client's address and `X-Forwarded-Proto: https`; a blind name under `t.H` and a terminated name under the blind zone receive the alert of protocol.md 6.2 (there is no wildcard certificate and no wildcard zone).
12. With a TLS intercepting proxy in the harness, the node connects, the proxy sees only ciphertext after the upgrade, and an injected record is rejected.
13. Unknown SNI, no SNI, bytes that are not TLS, quarantined names, a terminated share asked for as a blind name, an `acme-tls/1` hello for a share that did not list it, a node that refuses the stream, a name over its figure, a tenant over its figure and a node that is gone each get the same seven bytes, not sooner than the failure delay; a ClientHello fragmented up to 16 KiB is routed; a peek that exceeds the deadline or the cap is closed without a byte.
14. Relay static key rotation: nodes with the old key still connect for 30 days, receive the new NodeCert, and afterwards reject a lower relay serial.
15. WELCOME `rosters`: a stranger with a fresh key sees no network, in IK and in XX; an IK member sees exactly the networks it claimed, not every network that lists it; an XX member sees none, and its Roster upload is adopted or dropped as equal.
16. A HELLO that claims a network gets a byte identical ERROR whether the relay does not serve that network or serves it without listing the node; a HELLO that claims no network is refused; a DOC for an unserved network, for a served network the session did not claim, and a forged one all get no record back.
17. An owner who lists another tenant's node in its own Roster gets the same `peer_offline` whether that node is online, offline, or closed to peers, and the node receives nothing; members of a network both sides claimed still reach each other; every refused reliable SEND is reimbursed.
18. A tenant never gets a name in another tenant's zone, and the answer to its ADVERTISE does not depend on what another tenant holds; a released flat name and a removed tenant's slug stay quarantined across a relay restart.
19. One tenant at its figures does not take a second tenant's sessions, pairs or throughput: the 65th attached node of a tenant is refused while another tenant's first is accepted, a new pair over the figure is refused while existing pairs work, and the second tenant's transfer rate is not reduced by the first tenant's stalled credit. At the default floor a 16 MiB transfer completes and no session ends.
20. A renewal with the same keys, listed by the owner or by a steward, keeps every live session, and sessions on it outlive the older certificate's `not_after`; a renewal nobody lists ends the node's sessions at that `not_after`, and a late listing brings it back. A steward signed Policy that lists another key for a base member, drops or lowers a renewal the verifier holds, lists a second certificate for a node, or rotates without both `admit` and `renew` is refused; owner and steward refuse a renewal with a new key, a serial not above the listed one, or one valid for less than 5 minutes.
21. A pushed `node_cert` is never adopted on trust: one for another node than the session's peer, for another static key than the session's, with a new key, a bad signature, a window over 400 days, or a serial not above the highest known is refused, and every receiver gives the same answer whether it holds a steward key, the owner key or neither, so the push tells no one where the steward lives.
22. The relay principal's `init` is answered with silence when its static key is not the relay's key of the current L3 attachment (also when a member puts the relay's NodeId in its own `init`), when the node holds no accepted share for that network, no documents for it, expired documents, a revoked standing, or its own certificate is not in the Policy; and it is answered, with `policy_seq` 0, only when all hold.
23. A share the node withdraws resets its live stream and leaves the session and the other shares alone, and a new OPEN of it is `no_grant`; an L3 reconnect of the node ends the principal session and its streams; a frame of an older L3 session of the node never reaches a principal session; a reliable frame to the relay beyond its credit ends the sender's L3 session with ERROR `credit_exceeded`.
24. The terminator refuses, with the status of protocol.md 6.3 and without reaching the node: `Content-Length` with `Transfer-Encoding` in both orders, a `Transfer-Encoding` that is not exactly `chunked`, a bad, signed, hexadecimal, empty, list or doubled `Content-Length`, folded lines, bare LF or CR, a NUL or other control byte, a request that is not HTTP/1.1 in origin form, a duplicated or malformed `Host`, `Expect` and `Upgrade`, a `Connection` that names a framing header or `Host`, and a malformed chunked body; a response that would split or confuse framing is a 502.
25. A `Set-Cookie` from a terminated share keeps a `Domain` only if it is exactly the share's name; every parent, the share domain, `t.H` and a tenant zone is removed.
26. The ClientHello parser refuses every ambiguity of protocol.md 6.2 and parses a real rustls hello at every fragmentation; reading stops at 16 KiB, a stalled peer is dropped at the deadline and a peer that closes early is dropped.
27. A name resolves only while it is held, granted by the Roster now, held by an attached node, its network not held back and its tenant and zone agree: a label the owner moved or dropped, a holder the Roster no longer lists, a network that changed tenant and a quarantined name route nothing; the blind and the terminated zone never resolve each other's names.
28. The relay answers `acme-tls/1` only for a name with an order pending now and ALPN exactly `acme-tls/1`, and every other hello gets the same bytes as without ACME; no order is started for a name no accepted share of a flagged tenant makes wanted (a blind share is never ordered), one order per name per minute, doubling after failures to an hour, 16 in flight; the node side answers a challenge only for an exact pending name.
29. Share connections hold their figures: per name, per tenant, in all, per client per name and in all, per IPv6 /48, and every place is given back however the connection ends; a pipe the node never answers ends at the first byte timeout whatever the client sends.
