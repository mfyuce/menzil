# Red team review of the phase 0 spec (v0.1)

Produced 2026-09-26 by an independent review agent (Opus tier, read only) against the spec drafts at commit 1765b4b. Kept unedited as review provenance. Every finding numbered here is addressed or explicitly deferred in protocol.md v0.2 (see its changelog).

Red team review of docs/spec in /home/fatihyuce/work/projects/tmp/tunnel (protocol.md, threat-model.md, decisions/0001). Bare section numbers refer to protocol.md; TM means threat-model.md.

## Findings

### Critical

**1. The L4 replay window lets the relay silently delete or reorder stream data.** 5.2 ("accept out of order records inside it"), 5.3 ("body = one yamux frame"); TM 2.3.
Attack: (1) A sends B records n and n+1, each one yamux DATA frame of stream s. (2) The relay drops n, or swaps n and n+1. (3) B's window accepts both. yamux has no sequence numbers and assumes an ordered, reliable transport (yamux spec), and because records align with frames its parser never notices. (4) Stream s delivers a hole or reordered bytes. Plain TCP services (HTTP, unencrypted Postgres, file copies) never notice. An honest relay that drops under memory pressure causes the same damage. The teardown rule in 5.2 has nothing that would detect the loss.
Fix: "MUX, CLOSE and KEEP records form a reliable sequence: accept only counter == last + 1; any gap, duplicate or reordering closes the session and resets every stream." Droppable datagrams (finding 8) get a separate nonce class (the top counter bit), and only that class keeps the 2048 window. When L4 ends abnormally, close local sockets with RST, never FIN, so truncation is visible. Add a TM 5 test for drop, duplicate and reorder.

**2. Invite redemption hands the secret to the relay, so the relay can mint members.** 7.2 (contradicts its own last sentence and TM 2.3).
Attack: (1) The device sends INVITE_REDEEM{invite, secret, node_cert_D}. (2) The relay forwards the invite and secret with node_cert_R, a puppet it controls; nothing binds the secret to the NodeCert. (3) The owner node auto-signs a MemberCert for R with the invite's roles. (4) The relay answers owner_offline. R can now reach every service granted to those roles. Variant: keep secrets seen during owner_offline and redeem them first later. Use counts are checked per relay, so N relays allow N times the uses.
Fix: the secret never leaves the device. Send ADMIT_REQUEST{invite, node_cert, tag = keyed BLAKE2s(secret, "menzil.v1.admit" || invite_id || node_cert bytes)}. The owner verifies the tag, expiry and use count. The relay only checks the invite signature and may hold the request until the owner is online. Renewal runs over L4 to the owner. Delete e2e_proto 0x00 and the undefined "control envelope".

### High

**3. Authorization is not scoped to one network.** 2.2, 5.1, 5.3, 8.
Attack: B is in its own network N1 and in a friend's network N2. N2's owner gives A the role "admin". A reaches B through N2, and B applies N1's grant of tcp:ssh to role admin. Role names are free strings, so the owner of any network B shares can escalate into every network B belongs to. Egress rules have the same problem.
Fix: the initiator names network_id in msg 1 and the prologue. Membership, roles, grants and egress come only from that network's Policy. Principals are (network_id, NodeId or role).

**4. The relay can freeze revocation, and removed members keep access.** 2.2, 7.1, 7.3; TM 2.3 ("cannot change policy"), 2.5, 5.3.
Attack: stolen key K is revoked at seq 12. A malicious relay, or one restored from backup, never delivers seq 12 and keeps forwarding for K. Peers accept K indefinitely, and a reinstalled node accepts seq 3. Separately, a member dropped from `members` but not added to `revoked` keeps a MemberCert that the relay and 5.1 accept for up to 90 days. Stale MemberCert.roles also defeat demotion.
Fix: make the Policy the single source of truth: a member is listed and not revoked, roles come only from the Policy, and MemberCert is used only for HELLO admission (or dropped). Give the Policy `expires` (for example issued + 14 days, re-signed automatically by the owner node); peers fail closed once it passes. L4 handshakes carry each side's seq, and the side with the older one fetches the newer Policy end to end. The owner pushes revocations over L4 with acknowledgements. Add `certs` to the 2.2 schema, since 5.1 and 7.1 rely on it.

**5. Peer identity is not bound, and rotated X25519 keys stay valid for 400 days.** 2.1, 5.1; TM 2.5.
Attack A (misbinding): member M self-signs NodeCert{node_id: M, x25519: X_B}. A dials M, encrypting to X_B. M resends A's message to B as its own SEND, and B answers. A checks only "static key matches NodeCert", accepts B's cert, and believes it is talking to M.
Attack B (stale key): after B rotates a leaked X25519 key, the attacker presents B's old, unexpired NodeCert with the old key. The relay's HELLO check and the peers' 5.1 check both pass.
Fix: the initiator MUST check that the responder's node_id equals the NodeId it dialed. The responder MUST check that the initiator's node_id equals the RECV src. Set the prologue to "menzil.v1.e2e" || src || dst || network_id. NodeCert gains a `serial`, and the highest seen wins; the Policy carries a per-member `min_serial` that the owner raises on compromise. The owner refuses duplicate X25519 keys.

**6. Share streams opened by the relay have no authorization rule.** 6.1, 6.2 versus 5.1; 4.3.
Problem: the relay "opens an L4 stream" to the node, but 5.1 requires a common network, and a friend's relay is not a member. The obvious workaround, "trust my relay", lets a malicious relay OPEN tcp:ssh or egress and forge meta.client_ip.
Fix: the relay principal is the `id` of a relay the node has an L3 session with, never a `relay` role. It may open only tls:, http: and acme-http: for names in that relay's ADVERTISE_ACK, with a null target. `meta` is honored only from the relay principal. Relays refuse member traffic to nodes with accept_peers false.

**7. A recorded HELLO disconnects a node for months.** 4.1, 4.2 ("A new HELLO ... replaces the old session"); TM 2.2, 2.3 ("replay ... harmless").
Knowing a NodeId is not enough, because msg 1 needs the node's static key. But IK message 1 is "vulnerable to replay" (Noise spec).
Attack: (1) the inspecting proxy, or anyone reading its DLP logs, keeps one HELLO ciphertext. (2) From anywhere, the attacker opens a WebSocket and sends it. (3) The relay validates it, answers WELCOME and supersedes the live session. (4) The attacker repeats this after every reconnect, 10 times a minute per IP, for as long as the certificates inside stay valid (up to 90 days). With the relay's static key, KCI lets an attacker forge a HELLO for any node.
Fix: a session supersedes the old one and becomes routable only after the relay receives the node's first transport record (ATTACH). Add a monotonic timestamp to HELLO and reject any value not above the last one accepted for that NodeId (WireGuard's defense).

**8. Relay backpressure is unspecified, so one slow or malicious member stalls others.** 4.2 ("never buffers ... beyond the socket send buffer"), 5.4, 10.
Scenario: exit E streams to laptop L on hotel wifi, using many 256 KiB windows plus DGRAMs without flow control, and L's relay socket fills. The relay then has two choices, both bad. If it stops reading E, E's traffic to every peer stalls; E's pings go unanswered, so E declares the link dead after 10 s and loses all its L4 sessions. If it drops records, finding 1 applies. A malicious member can do this on purpose: advertise huge windows, then stop reading.
Fix: bounded queues per (source, destination). No session's reader ever blocks on another session. L3 credit flow control (CREDIT{peer, bytes}). A droppable flag in SEND for datagrams, which are dropped first. Senders prioritize control, then MUX, then DGRAM.

**9. L4 framing cannot be implemented as written.** 5.1, 5.2.
Gaps: nothing distinguishes handshake messages from transport records. There is no session or receiver index, yet rekey uses "a new session id". "2^60 records, 2^30 records" contradicts itself. Simultaneous open, restart, paths over several relays and key switch-over are undefined.
Fix: a WireGuard-style header (type, receiver index, counter). A responder keeps the current session until a data record confirms the new one, so a replayed msg 1 cannot reset it. On simultaneous open, keep the session started by the lower NodeId. Pin each session to the relay its msg 1 used. Rekey inside the session with Noise REKEY(), announced by a record, every 2^20 records or hourly. No application data in msg 1.

**10. One session per NodeId breaks the CLI, especially SSH ProxyCommand.** 1 ("the CLI in client mode is a node too"), 4.2, 11.
Scenario: two ssh sessions spawn two `menzil stdio` processes with one identity, and VS Code Remote-SSH and JetBrains Gateway open several at once. Each HELLO supersedes the other, and both sessions flap.
Fix: exactly one process per identity (a per-user agent) owns the relay and L4 sessions. CLI commands attach to it over local IPC restricted to that user.

**11. Egress is open to DNS rebinding, and the default deny list is incomplete.** 8; TM 5.5.
Attack: a grantee asks for rebind.example:80; the exit dials by name and gets 192.168.1.1 or 127.0.0.1. Literals the list misses: 0.0.0.0 (reaches localhost on Linux), ::ffff:127.0.0.1, 100.64.0.0/10, and the exit's own public address. Also, "unless the owner grants otherwise" contradicts "deny before allow".
Fix: canonicalize literals, match names, resolve once, check each address against deny and then allow, and dial only vetted addresses. Write the default list into the Policy at creation, so what is enforced is exactly what is signed: 0/8, 10/8, 100.64/10, 127/8, 169.254/16, 172.16/12, 192.168/16, 198.18/15, 224/4, 240/4, ::/128, ::1, fc00::/7, fe80::/10, ff00::/8, IPv4 embedded in 64:ff9b::/96, 64:ff9b:1::/48 and 2002::/16, and the exit's own addresses. UDP channels stay fixed to the OPEN target.

**12. Blind mode is not blind against an active relay, and a wildcard certificate leaks through connection coalescing.** 6.1, 6.2; TM 1, 2.3, 3.
Attack: the operator controls DNS for relay.example, gets a certificate for app.relay.example through DNS-01, and terminates the share itself. For custom domains pointed at the relay, HTTP-01 and TLS-ALPN-01 succeed for whoever receives the traffic; Let's Encrypt documents this for CNAMEd domains.
Passive leak: terminated mode's *.relay.example wildcard also covers blind names on the same IP. Browsers may therefore reuse an existing HTTP/2 connection and send app.relay.example requests, cookies included, to the relay's TLS terminator.
Fix: the TM should say: "blind against a passive relay; misissuance is detectable through CT and preventable only for custom domains with CAA accounturi (RFC 8657)". The relay never holds a certificate that covers a blind name (use a separate zone such as *.t.relay.example), and it answers 421 for any authority it does not terminate.

### Medium

**13. Relay key rotation has no mechanism.** 3.2, 4.1; TM 5.8 cannot be tested. Rotating the key strands every node, and a leaked old key is never revoked. Fix: see answer 1. Connection information must arrive out of band, never fetched through the intercepted L1 path.

**14. Size limits are wrong.** 3.3, 5.4, 4.1. Noise messages are at most 65,535 bytes. The DGRAM maximum of 65,467 leaves out the L4 counter and kind byte; the correct figure is 65,458, and lower once finding 9's header is added. Write "WebSocket message payload at most 65,535". WELCOME carries every policy, and a Policy with certs exceeds 64 KiB at a few hundred members. Send chunked POLICY records after the handshake instead.

**15. Most reconnects are self-inflicted.** 3.3, 4.1, 10. The 25 s ping fits common 60 s and 100 s idle timeouts; the failures are elsewhere:
- The proxy can answer or swallow WebSocket pings.
- On slow links, a pong queued behind megabytes of buffered data misses the 10 s deadline.
- Section 10's "idle without pong" contradicts "relay sends no pings".
- L3 rekey "on a new connection" kills every L4 session at least once a day.
Fix: L3 PING/PONG and REKEY records (in-band Noise REKEY() hourly). A link is dead only when no authenticated record arrives for two intervals. The relay treats a session as idle after 60 s with no frame. Use TCP_NOTSENT_LOWAT and give control records priority.

**16. Terminated mode HTTP handling.** 6.2. "Adds X-Forwarded-For" lets clients spoof their IP with their own header or a pipelined request. Fix: parse every request; strip Forwarded, X-Forwarded-* and X-Real-IP and set fresh values. Host must equal the SNI name, otherwise answer 421. Specify HTTP/2 to HTTP/1.1 translation (reject CR, LF and NUL, and conflicting Content-Length and Transfer-Encoding) and Upgrade handling.

**17. Labels, custom domains, open proxy.** 4.3; the "ownership proof" TM 2.6 claims does not exist. First come, first served by NodeId allows:
- squatting on shared relays;
- takeover of expired names (OAuth redirects, webhooks);
- losing labels on reinstall;
- exhausting the domain's Let's Encrypt budget.
Tenants under one registrable domain are same-site, which enables cookie tossing. ADVERTISE may also map a public name to egress.
Fix: see answer 4. Add reserved names and a single LDH label. Cap new labels per network per week. Use a separate registrable domain for shares (on the PSL if multi-tenant). The TXT record may name a NetworkId, with exact name matching only. Limit share services to http:, tls: and tcp:, with finite per-name limits.

**18. SNI router.** 6.1, 6.3; TM 2.6, 5.7. A ClientHello may span several TLS records and TCP segments (RFC 8446 5.1). Reassemble up to 16 KiB with a 10 s deadline and a cap on concurrent peeks. TLS 1.2 routes the same way, and clients without SNI get the static site. ECH hides the name, so forbid ECH configs for shares in v1. Route HTTP-01 and ALPN acme-tls/1 by share mode. CT logs and certificate mismatches reveal share names, so the static-site fallback does not prevent enumeration; the TM should say share names are public.

**19. Floods.** 5.4. DGRAMs bypass flow control and relays are unlimited by default, so a grantee can saturate the victim's only relay connection. Any member can spam msg 1, which costs four DH operations and two signature checks each. Channel ids collide when both sides open channels, and OPEN_ACK has no `channel` field. Fix: a token bucket per channel, a per-peer handshake limit, channel ids split by opener, and a `channel` field in OPEN_ACK.

**20. Grants are checked only at OPEN.** 5.3. Streams stay open after a grant is removed or expires. Fix: re-evaluate open streams and channels on every Policy and at expiry.

**21. Signed-object hygiene.** 1, 2, 9. Problems:
- No domain separation across the four signed types.
- "Ignore unknown keys" without a type tag invites type confusion and fails open on future security fields.
- Duplicate keys cause parser differentials.
- `Signed{}` has no defined byte encoding.
Fix: `Signed = [bstr body, bstr sig]`, signed over "menzil.v1." || type || 0x00 || body. Decode signed bodies strictly, forward signed bytes verbatim, and name the secret_hash function.

**22. The carrier in the target environment.** 3.1, 10.
- NTLM needs every 407 leg on one kept-alive connection, with response bodies drained. Kerberos needs the proxy's FQDN as the SPN.
- Answering a WPAD-discovered proxy with logged-on credentials leaks NTLM responses that can be relayed. Follow the OS automatic logon policy.
- PAC is untrusted JavaScript; bound its time and memory.
- Requiring TLS 1.3 at L1, which is untrusted anyway, breaks interception appliances that speak 1.2. Accept 1.2.
- An office behind one proxy shares a single HELLO budget. Raise the per-IP burst, limit per NodeId after the DH, and spread retry_after_ms on restarts.

**23. The relay sees the whole Policy.** 2.2, 7.1; TM 2.3. Grants, device names and egress host patterns are plaintext at the relay, and nothing restricts delivery to listed members. Fix: a Roster the relay can see (ids, revoked, min_serial), separate from a Policy delivered end to end, or admit this in the TM.

**24. Owner key.** The TM has no "stolen owner key" entry, and that case is unrecoverable. Invites keep the owner key on an online node that signs automatically. Two owner devices can issue the same seq. WELCOME `time` must never extend validity. Fix: add the TM entry, use a delegated admission key limited in roles and lifetime, and adopt a single-writer rule.

**25. Inconsistencies between the documents.**
- The protocol marks PAC, WPAD, NTLM, Negotiate, the blind-mode default, `menzil proxy`, datagrams and the static site as phase 1; review section 8 puts them in phases 2 and 3.
- The plan's phase 2 "ACLs" must not mean phase 1 ships without default-deny grants.
- Phase 2 "stream resumption" contradicts consequence 1 of ADR 0001.
- TM 2.8 contradicts ADR consequence 2 and yamux's ordering requirement.
- e2e_proto 0x00 is used although other values are "rejected".
- The label-only wildcard contradicts `["*"]` in TM 2.7.
- TM 2.5 says revocation applies at the "next session"; 7.3 says at the "next Policy".
Fix: a phase marker on each section.

### Low and Nit

**26. Low and nits.**
- Bind version negotiation: put the offered and selected subprotocols in the L3 prologue, and the supported e2e_proto tags in L4 payloads.
- State that X25519 without a post-quantum component is a v1 non-goal, since a logging proxy can harvest traffic now.
- `target` must be null outside egress, and `tcp:<port>` must not map implicitly to localhost.
- Show full NodeIds, or fingerprints of at least 80 bits, in admit prompts.
- On Windows, protect the key with an ACL; "mode 0600" means nothing there.
- Undefined: session_id, caps, max_peers, alpn, KEEP cadence, revoked.since, error codes.
- Nit: 4.1's check should read "NodeCert.x25519_pub equals the remote static key".
- Nit: `egress` breaks the `kind ":" label` grammar.
- Nit: the Rust yamux crate auto-tunes windows, so state whether 256 KiB is the initial or a fixed size.

## Answers to the open items

1. Protocol 12.1 and TM 6.1: yes, anchor the relay in its Ed25519 NodeId. WELCOME carries the relay's NodeCert with a serial. The node checks node_id against the connection info and x25519 against the static key used in the handshake. Keep `key` as a cache for IK; when it is missing or stale, fall back to Noise XX, which keeps the node's identity hidden. During rotation the relay accepts both static keys and returns the new NodeCert, and nodes never accept a lower serial.
2. 12.2: not as written. The replay window is what enables finding 1, and ADR 0001 already moves the direct path to QUIC. Use implicit nonces at L3. At L4, use explicit counters with contiguity required for reliable records, and a windowed class only for droppable datagrams.
3. 12.3: not in its current form. Ship the MAC-bound admission request that the relay stores and forwards; it also removes owner_offline and e2e_proto 0x00. The minimum for phase 1 is `menzil admit` with a pasted token.
4. 12.4: bind labels to the network through claims signed by the owner, with operator quotas per network. Release a label only by an owner-signed release or after 90 idle days, then quarantine it for 180 days. On a personal relay, configure labels statically.
5. 12.5: the ceiling is window divided by RTT. 256 KiB at 150 ms is about 14 Mbit/s per stream, and at 300 ms about 7 Mbit/s: fine for SSH, poor for bulk transfer. Do not negotiate the window in OPEN; yamux already lets the receiver enlarge it with a WINDOW_UPDATE at SYN/ACK. Auto-tune up to 16 MiB per stream within a per-session budget, once finding 8's credits exist.
6. TM 6.2: accept NodeId exposure. Routing needs it, and pseudonyms would not hide who talks to whom. Do hide grants, names and egress rules (finding 23).
7. TM 6.3: yes, by default. Terminated mode should need opt-in per share by the node and per network by the operator, with a warning. Phase 1 ships only terminated mode, so the TM must say that in phase 1 the relay can read shares.

## Verdict

Yes, after the listed fixes, but not as it stands. Blocking before any phase 1 code: findings 1 to 10 and the phase markers in 25, because they change wire formats or trust rules that phase 1 builds. Findings 11, 12, 17 and 18 block egress, blind shares and labels whenever those ship. The rest can be fixed alongside the code.
